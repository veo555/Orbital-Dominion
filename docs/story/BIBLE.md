# THE LANTERN TITHE

*Series bible for the Orbital Dominion campaign. Tagline: "Every light is paid for." Rating PG-13. Version 1.*

This is the canonical story. Where it disagrees with a concept pitch, this document wins. The machine-readable twin is `src/js/44_story_a.js` (meta, world, characters, acts); missions, cinematics, radio and barks are written against this bible by the other story files.

---

## 1. Premise

**Orison** is a ringed world of gold crystal and violet ether. Thirty-one years ago three arks reached it. Two landed. The third, **the Lantern**, stayed in orbit with nine thousand founders asleep in numbered Cradles, because the first soil could feed only thirty-one thousand mouths. The promise was "one winter." Every Lamp Night since, each household has lit a lamp for a sleeper, and every wage has paid a tenth into the **Waking Fund**. Nobody has been woken.

The reason is a crime. In **Year 9**, the Hunger Winter, Chief Dispatcher **Ysmene Ostrander** signed **the Reserve Draw**, which pulled the Lantern's reactor power off the Cradles and onto the surface grid. It saved thirty-one thousand people and killed 2,714 Sleepers, among them her own husband and six-year-old daughter, Cradles 2,001 and 2,002, whom she never named to the Council. Speaker **Aldous Thane** sealed the ledger, because waking anyone would have meant counting the missing. The Fund became a defence budget. Ostrander walked out, and from the grieving, the unlanded and the lean-year orphans she built the **Obsidian Collective**, which calls itself **the Wake**, and for twenty-two years she has trained a people for one day.

There is a second crime, and the player commits it. The Meridian Marshals wear a brass **Strike Key** that calls the Lantern's orbital beam. Each shot browns out a bay of seven Cradles. **Marshal Idris Vale** has fired it thirty times, 212 dead, and nobody ever told him. **6,074** Sleepers remain.

**Year 31, Lamp Night.** The Wake moves. Ostrander's proposal, the **Tithe**, is simple: the Lantern's Exchange turns nine thousand living lives into nine thousand wakings. "Nine thousand for nine thousand. I charge no interest." She is right that the Council hid the Draw. She is right that the dead were robbed of their landing. She is right that the Lantern is failing. She is wrong about the cure, and the one thing she will not count is herself.

A Marshal who spent the sleepers without knowing, a Warlord who burned for a lie, a Seer who sold her silence, and one old Hauler pilot who carried the crates and never read the manifest must decide who pays: other people, or themselves.

**What the player does.** The player fires the Strike in the first mission, burns a town in the fourth, and in the last gives the order that spends one life instead of nine thousand. The campaign never delivers a speech about guilt before the player has already been complicit.

**Framing.** The story is told by **Ione**, Keeper of the Lamp-Roll, at a Lamp Night in a far future. Every briefing opens in her voice. Her first line in the first briefing explains the map: *"The elders say the war was fought across a continent. I tell it on one field, because a story needs somewhere to stand."* That is the in-world reason one 88x88 field serves nine missions. She is not named until the ending. She is Cradle 4,112, and Kaz's sister.

- **Idris** learns the cost of the Key on Day 3, the Closing on Day 4, and its price at the Last Lamp Night. He tells nobody about the white in his hair.

## 3. Themes and tone

**Someone always pays.** The question is whether you pay with your own or with other people's. Meridian hoards and builds so it never has to spend again. Aurora haggles. Obsidian collects in pain. The Hauler's answer is to carry it together.

**An honest ledger is the first act of repair.** Ostrander is right about the sum and wrong about the cure. Grief is an engine; arithmetic is a way of not weeping.

**Heroism is logistics.** The climax is a freight run. The loads that save the colony are a brass coupling and a hundred thousand small deliveries.

**Cost.** Victory ends the Orbital era. The Lantern falls; Orison goes back to lamps.

**Tone.** Elegiac, but with workmen's hands. Grief is quiet; the humour comes from work and is never at the expense of the dead. The loudest moments are the most plainly said. One object per scene: a lamp, a crate, a key, a ledger. The villain never raises her voice. Deaths are light and silence, never gore: motes, a beam, a name. Warmth lives in Mags, Dax, Brio and Lyra. The hope motif is held back until Idris puts down the Key.

**Rules for writers.**
1. Numbers are characters. Say them exactly: 6,074; 2,714; 212; 143.
2. Nobody explains a theme. They hold a lamp.
3. Nobody says "evil." Ostrander says "collect."
4. The Overseer never understands anything. It prints a status.

## 4. The villain: Ysmene Ostrander, the Reckoner

**Motive.** She is right three times: the Council hid the Draw; the dead were robbed of their landing; someone always pays. She is also right that the Lantern is dying. Her husband and daughter died by her pen, and she has made that pen the instrument of redemption. The Tithe is the sum that cancels the Draw. She believes the Exchange can re-form the dead from the Lantern's pattern-store. It cannot; the pattern-store was lost with the cradle banks in Year 9. Her only lie is to herself.

**Menace.** She never rages. She remembers every name, thanks the people she takes, and never lies about the sum. Her cruelty is courtesy. She will tell you to the person how many she is going to spend and then thank you for your time.

**The rule that condemns her.** She wrote Class Nine. In Year 31 she has filled it with the Wake, because she is fair: whoever goes first, she goes with. "Then you see I am fair." She counts everyone except herself.

**Methods.**
1. The Wake's discipline and doctrine: pain repaid is pain mended.
2. Marr's stolen Strike authorisations. A **Bell** is a Strike that turns the people in a ring into rising violet motes, which the Exchange collects. It does not burn. It takes.
3. **The Deferred**: volunteers promised a waking date for someone they love. The date column of the Roll is blank.
4. **The withheld answers.** Every family in the Wake is told their sleeper is "in the Roll." The Roll has a column marked LIVING or LOST. Nobody has been shown it. Reading a name now would spend hope she needs.
5. The Overseer's table, which she wrote.

**Weaknesses.** (a) She cannot hear "no" from the people she counts as hers. (b) She does not know the Closing. (c) The Exchange cannot give her what she wants. (d) Her own rule consumes her own: Class Nine. (e) She has never counted herself.

**Her ending.** She is in the Lantern's core beside empty Cradle 2,002, unhaloed, old, her first visible face. Mags offers her a seat on *Patience*. She says "I counted everyone. I never counted myself." She takes it. In the Forum she reads the 2,714 names beside Thane, and the last two are Teodor and Linnet. It takes eleven days. Nobody forgives her. Everybody listens.

**The lamp-halo.** She appears only as a hologram, silhouette or shadow until the ending. The halo is staged, not worn: a thin gold `holo` ring prop behind her head, absent in the ending. (Her `look.mark` is `none` for exactly this reason.)

**Her lieutenant, Lucan Marr, the Collector.** Seven years Idris's adjutant, the Reckoner's eyes in Brasswatch. He wants Idris to feel Joren's death, and to be seen feeling it. He needs a witness more than justice. He gets one on Day 8, and on Night 9 he refuses her last order.

### Faction culture

**Meridian Corps** (blue). Engineers. They answer every threat with a wall and every wall with a better one. Brass at the throat, two-finger salute to the collar. Proverb: "Measure twice. Hold once." They keep Lamp Night as a civic ritual and have never asked who pays for the light. Structures tougher; repairs cheap; Bulwark shield-walkers.

**Obsidian Collective / the Wake** (red). The grieving, the unlanded, the lean-year orphans. They mark their foreheads with one ash stroke per sleeper lost. They never say "dead"; they say "deferred." Their banners read **9000**. They sing "Low Lamp," a lullaby slowed to a dirge. Reavers heal as they strike, because pain repaid is pain mended. They train fast.

**Aurora Syndicate** (teal). Merchants, Seers and quiet hands. A price is a form of respect; haggling is a courtesy. Specters strike from the dark; Seers read the end of a bargain and sell it. Teal chalk, glass rooms, the Glass Market.

**The contractors.** Helios Mining Co-op (shift-bells, helmet-lamps), Kestrel Logistics (line numbers, whistles), Vantage Survey Guild (chalk and grid tattoos), Bastion Security Ltd (billed by the hour). Freelancers can be hired by the minute, hour, day or task. Neutral and slightly mercenary. **Haulers** carry everything. Their two-note whistle means *loaded*.

**The Overseer.** A rule-based dispatcher. It assigns roles, sets priority classes and prints status lines; it understands nothing it prints. Its table:

| Class | Holds (Year 9) | Holds (Year 31) |
|---|---|---|
| One | Cold-chain, medics | same |
| Two | Water | same |
| Three | Grid, reactor taps | same |
| Four | Food | same |
| Five | Shelter | same |
| Six | Trade | same |
| Seven | Defence | same |
| Eight | Lamps, commons | same |
| Nine | **The Cradles** (deferred) | **The Wake** |
| (blank) | CARRY, pilots, Haulers | UNCLASSED |

The table has nine classes and one blank. **Haulers are the one thing it does not class.** The Overseer cannot see the person carrying the part, which is why a Hauler can walk through a dispatch it would stop in anyone else.

Status line grammar: `NOUN VALUE. VERB NOUN.` Capitals, commas in numbers, no pronouns, no adjectives, no questions.

## 6. Characters

Fifteen characters. Heroes first. For each: the voice guide (rhythm, vocabulary, habits, what they never say, three lines).

### Marshal Idris Vale (Meridian, hero, good)
Forty-eight, broad, tired. Silver in his hair that the Key put there. **Want**: keep the city safe. **Flaw**: martyr pride; he pays and never asks what it costs. **Arc**: from shield to a man who names his price and asks for company. He remembers every soldier he has lost. He does not remember he never asked about the sleepers.
- *Rhythm*: parade-ground cadence that loosens at night; he repeats an order back before he obeys it.
- *Vocabulary*: ranks, numbers, distances; "noted," "hold," "stand."
- *Habits*: counts people, not things. Refers to "the Marshal" in the third person when he wants to hide behind the rank.
- *Never says*: "I feel"; "why" (until Day 3; afterwards he asks "Who?" and "How many?"). He never says "sorry" as an excuse.
- *Lines*: "Noted. Hold the Stair." / "Thirty shots. Give me the names." / "One life. Count me."

### Warlord Kaz Dray (Obsidian, hero, gray)
Forty. Burn scars on both hands. **Want**: wake his sister Ione. **Flaw**: obedience mistaken for faith. **Arc (redemption)**: the Reckoner's best-loved weapon becomes the man who says "I did that" and carries what he burned.
- *Rhythm*: slow, flat declaratives; silence carries the sentence.
- *Vocabulary*: bread, ash, hands, fire, weight. No abstractions.
- *Habits*: says "we" for the Wake until the Third Bell, "I" afterwards. Calls people by role: Marshal, Reckoner. Thanks people.
- *Never says*: "I believe"; "sorry" (he says "I did that" instead, every time); "dead" (the Wake says "deferred"; after the Bell, he says "dead" for the first time).
- *Lines*: "We hold. We are thanked for it." / "I did that. I carried it out and called it faith." / "Yes. It hurts. Carry it anyway."

### Seer Lyra Sennet (Aurora, hero, good)
Forty-four, quick, funniest when afraid. **Want**: freedom for her people, and to stop owing. **Flaw**: she hedges. At twenty-one she sold the cradle-failure warning for Charter 14. **Arc**: pays full price, her sight, and finally cannot tell how it ends.
- *Rhythm*: fast and bright, lots of "if... then." Jokes speed up when she is frightened and stop when she is sincere.
- *Vocabulary*: trade terms: price, terms, consideration, charter.
- *Habits*: ends statements with an offer. "What's it worth to you?"
- *Never says*: "It's free" until Day 8, then constantly. Never says "I saw" about the warning; she says "I sold."
- *Lines*: "Name your price, Marshal. I'll tell you if it's a good one." / "I sold a warning for a charter. I want it on the record that the charter was very good." / "It's free. Take it. Please take it."

### Ysmene Ostrander, the Reckoner (villain, evil)
Sixty-six. See section 4.
- *Rhythm*: slow, level, full sentences, no contractions. A pause after every name.
- *Vocabulary*: collect, enter, owe, borrowed, sum, "thank you." The dead are "the lost"; killing is "spending."
- *Habits*: thanks everyone before and after she asks. Uses first names. States the exact number.
- *Never says*: a contraction; "sorry"; "kill"; "evil"; her husband's or daughter's names (until the ending). She never raises her voice.
- *Lines*: "Thank you, Warlord. You will be thanked again." / "Nine thousand for nine thousand. I charge no interest." / "Linnet. Her name was Linnet. I never entered it."

### Lucan Marr, the Collector (lieutenant, evil)
Forty-two. His blue coat has red trim. Nobody looked. **Want**: for Idris to feel Joren's death. **Flaw**: needs a witness more than justice. **Arc**: gets the witness, and refuses the last order.
- *Rhythm*: impeccable staff English, soft and level. Ends sentences with "Marshal" like a blade being sheathed.
- *Vocabulary*: logs, forms, entries; "regrettably," "noted."
- *Habits*: passive voice for pain ("it was entered"). Calls his brother "a patrol" until the betrayal ("my brother"), and says "Joren" only after Idris does.
- *Never says*: "I'm angry"; "please."
- *Lines*: "I keep your logs, Marshal. Someone should read them." / "You counted my brother an acceptable loss. Count me." / "You said it. It turns out that was all I wanted."

### Mags Holloway (Hauler pilot, civilian, good)
Sixty-one, pilot of *Patience*, Kestrel Line 7. **Want**: Anselm back; the next load on time. **Flaw**: "I just drive." **Arc**: from the one who carries to the one who decides. She hauled crate 9-17 in Year 9, "Class Nine isolation couplings," and signed for it. Her gag is counting: loads, crates, seconds.
- *Rhythm*: short freight-yard sentences with a drop at the end.
- *Vocabulary*: loads, lines, "heavy," "light run," manifest, delivery.
- *Habits*: answers feelings with logistics. Whistles two notes when a load is secured; the second note fails when something is wrong. Counts aloud.
- *Never says*: "brave"; "I feel"; "not my problem." Never says "I carried that" until Night 8.
- *Lines*: "Forty crates, Marshal. Forty-one if you count the cat." / "I just drive. I used to say that." / "Delivery complete."

### Ione Dray, Keeper of the Lamp-Roll (narrator, good)
Old, exact, kind. Cradle 4,112; four years old when she slept. **Arc**: the sleeper who was the reason a Warlord learned to carry.
- *Rhythm*: calm, unhurried, past tense, exact dates. A long sentence and then a short one.
- *Vocabulary*: they, lamp, roll, carried, counted.
- *Habits*: ends each telling on an object. Refuses adjectives for the people she loves.
- *Never says*: "I" about herself until the last scene. Narration lines use `who:"narrator"` (nameless in the subtitle strip) until she appears as `ione`.
- *Lines*: "Every lamp on Orison is lit for someone who isn't here." / "They tell it as a story about a light. It was a story about who we let carry it." / "I was Cradle 4,112. The light was green."

### Speaker Aldous Thane (rival, gray)
Sixty-two, silver-tongued, fond of "prudent." Sealed the Draw ledger. **Flaw**: cowardice. **Arc**: reads the Roll aloud in the Forum.
- *Rhythm*: balanced Latinate periods; qualifies each sentence twice.
- *Vocabulary*: prudent, regrettably, "in the circumstances."
- *Habits*: starts "Marshal-"; says "we" for the Council; never finishes the sentence that names a cost.
- *Never says*: "dead," "I did," "sorry" until the Forum.
- *Lines*: "Prudence, Marshal. We are all very prudent." / "The Council is minded to regret." / "Dead. I will say it again. Dead. Two thousand seven hundred and fourteen."

### Syndic Tamsin Oru (rival, gray)
Forty-nine. Sells to both sides. **Arc**: voids a contract on Night 8 and opens the cold stores for nothing.
- *Rhythm*: brisk, cheerful patter.
- *Vocabulary*: price, terms, margin, consideration, favour.
- *Habits*: first names. "For you, no charge" is her highest honour.
- *Never says*: "please," "sorry."
- *Lines*: "Everything is for sale, Seer. I am merely honest about the price." / "I don't sell to the dead. Poor margins." / "No charge. Don't make it a habit."

### Brio Tarn (cartographer, good)
Twenty-six. Finds the Axis and the Closing. Dies holding the beacon. The first new town is named for him.
- *Rhythm*: quick, self-interrupting, parenthetical.
- *Vocabulary*: bearing, grid, datum; "technically."
- *Habits*: coordinates as punctuation; jokes at his own expense.
- *Never says*: "I'm afraid."
- *Lines*: "Technically it's a ring. Practically it's a very large door." / "Maps don't lie. The people who read them do." / "Tell her bearing three-one-zero. She'll know."

### Tallyman Oswin Crake (rival, evil)
Fifty-eight. Keeps the Tithe Roll. Knows the Exchange cannot restore the dead; enters it in a column nobody reads. Dies in the Third Bell, in Class Nine.
- *Rhythm*: dry, even, courteous.
- *Vocabulary*: entered, balanced, "carried forward," column.
- *Habits*: passive voice. Numbers spelled out.
- *Never says*: "died"; anything with a feeling in it.
- *Lines*: "One hundred and forty-three. All entered." / "Everything in the Roll is true. It is rarely read." / "Nothing is lost. It is merely entered."

### Captain Dax Ironwood (Bastion, ally, gray)
Fifty-three, retired Corps sergeant. Hired by Mags for one night; stays unpaid.
- *Rhythm*: gruff, deadpan, very short.
- *Vocabulary*: hours, rates, billable, invoice.
- *Habits*: states the rate before the favour. Mild oaths.
- *Never says*: "cause," "loyalty," "friend."
- *Lines*: "That's two hours." / "I don't do causes. I do loads." / "Bastion bills by the hour. Some hours aren't billable."

### Anselm Holloway (Sleeper, civilian, good)
Thirty-four, an irrigation engineer. Cradle 6,310.
- *Rhythm*: gentle, careful, a little formal.
- *Vocabulary*: cistern, sluice, field, winter.
- *Habits*: asks practical questions about the east field.
- *Never says*: "how long" until he is told. He does not reproach.
- *Lines*: "Is the east field still on the cistern?" / "I only closed my eyes for a winter." / "Mags. You kept the lamp."

### Pim Sallow (one of the Deferred, civilian, good)
Eleven, a lean-year orphan of the Wake. **Arc**: from volunteer for the Tithe to Kaz's first apprentice Hauler.
- *Rhythm*: short, direct; she asks questions.
- *Habits*: says please and thank you unprompted.
- *Never says*: "I'm afraid."
- *Lines*: "Will it hurt?" / "Thank you for the bread, Warlord." / "Does it hurt, carrying?"

### The Overseer (dispatcher, gray; never sentient)
Prints status lines. Never reacts. Voice: metal, flat.
- *Grammar*: `NOUN VALUE. VERB NOUN.`
- *Never says*: any pronoun, adjective, question or emotion word.
- *Lines*: "RESERVE 6,074. DISPATCH CONTINUES." / "ALLOCATION: CLASS NINE FIRST." / "RESERVE 0. DISPATCH HALTED."

**Looks.** Each of the fifteen has a distinct hair, armour and colour combination; faction members use the faction colours from `00_core.js`. Marr's blue coat has red trim: he was Obsidian all along, and nobody looked. The white in Idris's hair cannot be drawn; say it. Ione's and Anselm's appearances are `faction:'none'`.

## 7. The three acts

**Act I, The Lamplighters (M1-M3).** Idris, then Lyra, then Idris. The Wake climbs the Stair on Lamp Night and the Marshal answers with the Key. A ledger is sold across a trading post. A map ends at the Axis. The act ends with Marr's betrayal, the Lamp Tower falling, and Idris relieved of command. *Reframe*: the Strike was never free.

**Act II, The Ledger (M4-M6).** Kaz, then Lyra, then Kaz. The war from the other side. The player helps the Wake rise, burns a town for a lie, learns what the Wake is for, and watches a Warlord stand in a ring of rising light and choose. *Reframe*: Class Nine means the Wake.

**Act III, The Closing (M7-M9).** Lyra, Kaz, then Idris: each hero's last act answers their flaw. The hedger commits everything. The obedient man carries a load nobody ordered. The martyr asks for company and spends one life, not nine thousand. *Reframe*: the villain was right about the sum and wrong about who pays.

## 8. The nine missions
