# THE LANTERN TITHE

*Series bible for the Orbital Dominion campaign. Tagline: "Every light is paid for." Rating PG-13. Version 2 (revised after the first critic pass; section 12 lists what changed and what is still unverified).*

This is the canonical story. Where it disagrees with a concept pitch, this document wins. The machine-readable twin is `src/js/44_story_a.js` (meta, world, characters, acts); missions, cinematics, radio and barks are written against this bible by the other story files.

---

## 1. Premise

**Orison** is a ringed world of gold crystal and violet ether. Thirty-one years ago three arks reached it. Two landed. The third, **the Lantern**, stayed in orbit with nine thousand founders asleep in numbered Cradles, because the first soil could feed only thirty-one thousand mouths. The promise was "one winter." Every Lamp Night since, each household has lit a lamp for a sleeper, and every wage has paid a tenth into the **Waking Fund**. Nobody has been woken.

The reason is a crime. In **Year 9**, the Hunger Winter, Chief Dispatcher **Ysmene Ostrander** signed **the Reserve Draw**, which pulled the Lantern's reactor power off the Cradles and onto the surface grid. It saved thirty-one thousand people and killed 2,714 Sleepers, among them her own husband and six-year-old daughter, Cradles 2,001 and 2,002, whom she never named to the Council. Speaker **Aldous Thane** sealed the ledger, because waking anyone would have meant counting the missing. The Fund became a defence budget. Ostrander walked out, and from the grieving, the unlanded and the lean-year orphans she built the **Obsidian Collective**, which calls itself **the Wake**, and for twenty-two years she has trained a people for one day.

There is a second crime, and the player commits it. The Meridian Marshals wear a brass **Strike Key** that calls the Lantern's orbital beam. Each shot browns out a bay of seven Cradles. **Marshal Idris Vale** has fired it thirty times, 212 dead, and nobody ever told him. **6,074** Sleepers remain on Lamp Night, and the Key takes seven more before the night is out.

**Year 31, Lamp Night.** The Wake moves. Ostrander's proposal, the **Tithe**, is simple: the Lantern's Exchange turns nine thousand living lives into nine thousand wakings. "Nine thousand for nine thousand. I charge no interest." She is right that the Council hid the Draw. She is right that the dead were robbed of their landing. She is right that the Lantern is failing. She is wrong about the cure, and the one thing she will not count is herself.

**Why the Wake climbs the Stair on Lamp Night.** A Bell needs a Marshal's authorisation, and the freshest one is the one signed on Lamp Night. Marr needs the Key to answer once more, in the Marshal's own hand, before he carries the whole case to the Spire. Kaz is told only to make the Key answer; his "Thank you for the light" is a courtesy he does not understand. i03 pays it off ("Every one the Marshal ever signed. And last night's.").

A Marshal who spent the sleepers without knowing, a Warlord who burned for a lie, a Seer who sold her silence, and one old Hauler pilot who carried the crates and never read the manifest must decide who pays: other people, or themselves.

**What the player does.** The player fires the Strike in the first mission (Idris says "Fire." on the radio whether or not the player has), burns a town in the fourth, and in the last gives the order that spends one life instead of nine thousand. The campaign never delivers a speech about guilt before the player has already been complicit.

**Framing.** The story is told by **Ione**, Keeper of the Lamp-Roll, at a Lamp Night in a far future. Every briefing opens in her voice. Her first line in the first briefing explains the map: *"The elders say the war was fought across a continent. I tell it on one field, because a story needs somewhere to stand."* That is the in-world reason one 88x88 field serves nine missions. The "I" there is the teller's "I" and is allowed; what she withholds until the last scene is the "I" about herself. She is not named until the ending. She is Cradle 4,112, and Kaz's sister.

### Who knows what

- **Idris** learns the cost of the Key on Day 3, the Closing on Day 4, and its price at the Last Lamp Night. He tells nobody about the white in his hair. He knew whose brother Marr was from the first day and never said it.
- **Kaz** never learns the Wake's own arithmetic until the Third Bell. He learns Ione is alive on Day 5 and obeys anyway. That is his flaw working.
- **Lyra** knows from Y8 what her silence cost. She is the first to know the Closing needs her, and the last to say so.
- **Mags** knows what she hauled. She says it on Night 8.
- **Ostrander** does not know the Closing exists until the machine refuses her. **Crake** knows the Exchange cannot restore the dead. **Marr** knows the Strike logs. Thane knows everything and says none of it.

## 3. Themes and tone

**Someone always pays.** The question is whether you pay with your own or with other people's. Meridian hoards and builds so it never has to spend again. Aurora haggles. Obsidian collects in pain. The Hauler's answer is to carry it together.

**An honest ledger is the first act of repair.** Ostrander is right about the sum and wrong about the cure. Grief is an engine; arithmetic is a way of not weeping.

**Heroism is logistics.** The climax is a freight run. The loads that save the colony are a brass coupling and a hundred thousand small deliveries.

**Cost.** Victory ends the Orbital era. The Lantern falls; Orison goes back to lamps. The Lantern has fed the surface grid since the Draw, so the fall is a winter chosen with eyes open (Oru's cold stores are enough; the ending shows them opened, no charge).

**Tone.** Elegiac, but with workmen's hands. Grief is quiet; the humour comes from work and is never at the expense of the dead. The loudest moments are the most plainly said. One object per scene: a lamp, a crate, a key, a ledger. The villain never raises her voice. Deaths are light and silence, never gore: motes, a beam, a name; coats and blades stay where the people were. Warmth lives in Mags, Dax, Brio and Lyra. The hope motif is held back until Idris puts down the Key.

**Rules for writers.**
1. Numbers are characters. Say them exactly: 6,074; 6,067; 2,714; 2,933; 212; 143; 38.
2. Nobody explains a theme. They hold a lamp.
3. Nobody says "evil." Ostrander says "collect."
4. The Overseer never understands anything. It prints a status.
5. **The maxim budget.** Only Ione's narration and the planted lines in section 3a may sound like an aphorism. Everybody else speaks plainly, in the moment, and is interrupted. Mags answers feeling with procedure, never with wisdom.
6. **Two heroes, two different flaws.** Kaz hands his conscience UP to a person he loves ("Yes, Reckoner"). Idris keeps his PRIVATE and pays alone ("Noted, Speaker"). Kaz is cured by saying "I did that" aloud and carrying. Idris is cured by saying a name aloud, asking for help, and letting his company stay.
7. **Do not stage every turn as somebody reading a document.** The M5 and M7 turns are physical (the Veil vision, a drawn blade lowered), not text on a hologram.

## 4. The villain: Ysmene Ostrander, the Reckoner

**Motive.** She is right three times: the Council hid the Draw; the dead were robbed of their landing; someone always pays. She is also right that the Lantern is dying. Her husband and daughter died by her pen, and she has made that pen the instrument of redemption. The Tithe is the sum that cancels the Draw. She believes the Exchange can re-form the dead from the Lantern's pattern-store. It cannot; the pattern-store was lost with the cradle banks in Year 9. Her only lie is to herself.

**Menace.** She never rages. She remembers every name, thanks the people she takes, and never lies about the sum. Her cruelty is courtesy. She will tell you to the person how many she is going to spend and then thank you for your time.

**The rule that condemns her.** She wrote Class Nine. In Year 31 she has filled it with the Wake, because she is fair: whoever goes first, she goes with. "Then you see I am fair." But the Roll has a line the Wake has not read: *Ostrander, Y.: exempt, essential.* It echoes her Year 0 exemption. Lyra finds it in M5 and asks it to her face in i07: "Are you in it?" She counts everyone except herself.

**Order of the Roll.** The Roll has two schedules and they run in order. **Removal** first: Class Seven, the Corps posts that could interrupt the Tithe (Bells One and Two). **Collection** second: Class Nine, the Collective, first among the collected (the Third Bell, and "ALLOCATION: CLASS NINE FIRST" in M9). Removal is by authorisation; Collection is "by lot, in daylight." A standing line, entered in advance, puts anyone who refuses an allocation into Class Nine: that is how Marr is entered in M9. The Overseer only executes it.

**Methods.**
1. The Wake's discipline and doctrine: pain repaid is pain mended.
2. Marr's stolen Strike authorisations. A **Bell** is a Strike that turns the people in a ring into rising violet motes, which the Exchange collects. It does not burn. It takes; coats and blades stay.
3. **The Deferred**: volunteers promised a waking date for someone they love. The date column of the Roll is blank.
4. **The withheld answers.** Every family in the Wake is told their sleeper is "in the Roll." The Roll has a column marked LIVING or LOST. Nobody has been shown it. Reading a name now would spend hope she needs. Lyra broadcasts it in M7.
5. The Overseer's table, which she wrote.

**Weaknesses.** (a) She cannot hear "no" from the people she counts as hers. (b) She does not know the Closing. (c) The Exchange cannot give her what she wants. (d) Her own rule consumes her own: Class Nine. (e) She has never counted herself.

**Her turn, witnessed (M9 debrief).** She watches Idris's sacrifice on her own feed, in a dark hall, in shadow (never a bust before the ending). Her numbers mind does the sum aloud: "One. For six thousand and sixty-seven." Then, quieter: "He said it plainly. I could not." Her last order to Marr in the mission, "Thank you, Lucan. Fire.", is hers, voiced, so his refusal is a refusal of her and not of a system.

**Why not her? (the question the audience will ask)** The Founders' text reads: *Whoever bears the Key and has spent the Lantern may pay the Lantern once.* Ostrander spent the Lantern with a pen; she never bore the Key. Lyra says it in i08 ("Hers was a pen. The Axis wants the Key."). In the ending Ostrander tries to take his place at the console, and the machine refuses: ACCOUNT CLOSED. NO BALANCE DUE. "It was written for me. It asked for the Key, and the Key was his." So she lives, accountable, which is harder than dying once.

**Her ending.** She is in the Lantern's core beside Cradle 2,002: "Linnet. Cradle two thousand and two. Empty since the winter I emptied it." (The child is dead; the cradle stands empty; the pod is the way down.) Unhaloed, old, her first visible face. She leaves in Linnet's pod before the Lantern falls; Mags: "There is a seat in that pod. I will carry you when you land." She says "I counted everyone. I never counted myself." In the Forum she reads the names beside Thane, her own family's in their turn at 2,001 and 2,002, without pausing. It takes eleven days. Nobody forgives her. Everybody listens.

**The lamp-halo.** She appears only as a hologram, silhouette or shadow until the ending. The halo is staged, not worn: a thin gold `holo` ring prop behind her head. It is the Wake's own icon of her, the sign the Deferred are shown, which is why its absence in the ending reads as the performance ending, not just a costume change. It never appears in Year 9. (Her `look.mark` is `none` for exactly this reason.)

**Her lieutenant, Lucan Marr, the Collector.** Seven years Idris's adjutant, the Reckoner's eyes in Brasswatch. Idris knew whose brother he was from the first day and never said it; that silence is Idris's flaw and exactly why Marr needs a witness. He wants Idris to feel Joren's death, and to be seen feeling it. He gets one on Day 8 when Idris says "Joren Marr" and the date. Idris then says "Brio Tarn" as well, and Marr flinches: one name-saying does not cover the second debt. On Night 9 he refuses her last order, and in the debrief gives Idris his logs ("Every one. Someone should read them.") and says "Joren." before the motes take him. He appears in Act II as the Bell operator (i03, m06_brief).

### Faction culture

**Meridian Corps** (blue). Engineers. They answer every threat with a wall and every wall with a better one. Brass at the throat, two-finger salute to the collar. Proverb: "Measure twice. Hold once." They keep Lamp Night as a civic ritual and have never asked who pays for the light. Structures tougher; repairs cheap; Bulwark shield-walkers.

**Obsidian Collective / the Wake** (red). The grieving, the unlanded, the lean-year orphans. They mark their foreheads with one ash stroke per sleeper lost. They never say "dead"; they say "deferred." Their banners read **9000**. They sing "Low Lamp," a lullaby slowed to a dirge. Reavers heal as they strike, because pain repaid is pain mended. They train fast.

**Aurora Syndicate** (teal). Merchants, Seers and quiet hands. A price is a form of respect; haggling is a courtesy. Specters strike from the dark; Seers read the end of a bargain and sell it. Teal chalk, glass rooms, the Glass Market. Over the Tollhouse door hangs the **Price Lamp**, lit only when a debt is paid in full; it has been dark since Year 8.

**The contractors.** Helios Mining Co-op (shift-bells, helmet-lamps), Kestrel Logistics (line numbers, whistles), Vantage Survey Guild (chalk and grid tattoos), Bastion Security Ltd (billed by the hour). Freelancers can be hired by the minute, hour, day or task. Neutral and slightly mercenary. **Haulers** carry everything. Their two-note whistle means *loaded*.

**The Overseer.** A rule-based dispatcher. It assigns roles, sets priority classes and prints status lines; it understands nothing it prints. Its table:

| Class | Holds (Year 9) | Holds (Year 31) |
|---|---|---|
| One | Cold-chain, medics | same |
| Two | Water | same |
| Three | Grid, reactor taps | same |
| Four | Food | same |
| Five | Shelter | same |
| Six | Trade | same |
| Seven | Defence | same (Removal schedule) |
| Eight | Lamps, commons | same |
| Nine | **The Cradles** (deferred) | **The Wake** (Collection schedule) |
| (blank) | CARRY, pilots, Haulers | UNCLASSED |

The table has nine classes and one blank. **Haulers are the one thing it does not class.** The Overseer cannot see the person carrying the part, which is why a Hauler can walk through a dispatch it would stop in anyone else, and why the beam cannot take Mags.

Status line grammar: `NOUN VALUE. VERB NOUN.` Capitals, commas in numbers, no pronouns, no adjectives, no questions.

## 6. Characters

Sixteen characters. Heroes first. For each: the voice guide (rhythm, vocabulary, habits, what they never say, three lines). **Token ownership** (so the clerical cluster does not blur): Crake owns *entered / carried forward / column / balanced*. Thane owns *prudent / regrettably / in the circumstances*. Marr owns concrete dates and names and the "Marshal" sheath; no abstractions; never "entered," never "regrettably." Ostrander owns *thank you*, first names, the future tense and *collect / spend / lost*. Idris owns *noted / hold / stand*.

### Marshal Idris Vale (Meridian, hero, good)
Forty-eight, broad, tired. Silver in his hair that the Key put there. **Want**: keep the city safe. **Flaw**: martyr pride; he pays in private and never asks anyone to help carry it, and he has known for seven years whose brother stands at his shoulder and never said so. **Arc**: from a shield who pays alone and asks nothing into a man who says the names aloud, asks for company for the first time, and chooses his price knowingly. He remembers every soldier he has lost. He does not remember he never asked about the sleepers.
- *Rhythm*: parade-ground cadence that loosens at night; he repeats an order back before he obeys it.
- *Vocabulary*: ranks, numbers, distances; "noted," "hold," "stand."
- *Habits*: counts people, not things (he says Cradle 6,310 back to Mags). Refers to "the Marshal" in the third person when he wants to hide behind the rank.
- *Never says*: "I feel"; "why" (until Day 3; afterwards he asks "Who?", "Who else?" and "How many?"). He never says "sorry" as an excuse.
- *Lines*: "Noted. Hold the Stair." / "Thirty shots. Give me the names." / "One life. Count me."

### Warlord Kaz Dray (Obsidian, hero, gray)
Forty. Burn scars on both hands. **Want**: wake his sister Ione. **Flaw**: obedience mistaken for faith; he hands his conscience UP to the person who fed him. **Arc (redemption)**: the Reckoner's best-loved weapon becomes the man who says "I did that" and carries what he burned, and carries Ione's pod down the field.
- *Rhythm*: slow, flat declaratives; silence carries the sentence.
- *Vocabulary*: bread, ash, hands, fire, weight. No abstractions.
- *Habits*: says "we" for the Wake until the Third Bell, "I" afterwards. Calls people by role: Marshal, Reckoner. Thanks people.
- *Never says*: "I believe"; "sorry" (he says "I did that" instead, every time); "dead" (the Wake says "deferred"; after the Bell, he says "dead" for the first time).
- *Lines*: "We hold. We are thanked for it." / "I did that. I carried it out and called it faith." / "Yes. It hurts. Carry it anyway."

### Seer Lyra Sennet (Aurora, hero, good)
Forty-four, quick, funniest when afraid. **Want**: freedom for her people, and to stop owing. **Flaw**: she hedges. At twenty-one she sold the cradle-failure warning for Charter 14. **Arc**: pays full price, her sight, and finally cannot tell how it ends.
- *Rhythm*: fast and bright, lots of "if... then." Jokes speed up when she is frightened and stop when she is sincere.
- *Vocabulary*: trade terms: price, terms, consideration, charter.
- *Habits*: ends statements with an offer. "What's it worth to you?" Makes a joke at the worst moment ("shoot back politely").
- *Never says*: "It's free" until Day 8, then constantly. Never says "I saw" about the warning; she says "I sold."
- *Lines*: "Name your price, Marshal. I'll tell you if it's a good one." / "I have a fleet and no army, which I'm told is just a very expensive parade." / "It's free. Take it. Please take it."

### Ysmene Ostrander, the Reckoner (villain, evil)
Sixty-six. See section 4.
- *Rhythm*: slow, level, full sentences, no contractions. A pause after every name. Present and future tense, second person.
- *Vocabulary*: collect, enter (only in the Roll sense, never in speech), owe, borrowed, sum, "thank you." The dead are "the lost"; killing is "spending."
- *Habits*: thanks everyone before and after she asks. Uses first names always. States the exact number.
- *Never says*: a contraction; "sorry"; "kill"; "evil"; her husband's or daughter's names (until the ending). She never raises her voice.
- *Lines*: "Thank you, Warlord. You will be thanked again." / "Nine thousand for nine thousand. I charge no interest." / "Linnet. Her name was Linnet. I never entered it."

### Lucan Marr, the Collector (lieutenant, evil)
Forty-two. His blue coat has red trim. Nobody looked. **Want**: for Idris to feel Joren's death. **Flaw**: needs a witness more than justice. **Arc**: gets the witness, still owes for Brio and the Tower, refuses the last order, and leaves the logs.
- *Rhythm*: impeccable staff English, soft and level. Ends sentences with "Marshal" like a blade being sheathed.
- *Vocabulary*: logs, dates, names, bays, forms; "logged," "noted."
- *Habits*: concrete dates and names, never abstractions. Calls his brother "a patrol" until the betrayal ("my brother"), and says "Joren" only after Idris does.
- *Never says*: "I'm angry"; "please"; "entered"; "regrettably."
- *Lines*: "I keep your logs, Marshal. Someone should read them." / "You counted my brother an acceptable loss. Count me." / "You said it. It turns out that was all I wanted."

### Mags Holloway (Hauler pilot, civilian, good)
Sixty-one, pilot of *Patience*, Kestrel Line 7. **Want**: Anselm back; the next load on time. **Flaw**: "I just drive." **Arc**: from the one who carries to the one who decides. ## 7. The three acts

**Act I, The Lamplighters (M1-M3).** Idris, then Lyra, then Idris. The Wake climbs the Stair on Lamp Night and the Marshal answers with the Key. A ledger is sold across a trading post. A map ends at the Axis. The act ends with Marr's betrayal, the Lamp Tower falling with thirty-eight keepers in it, and Idris relieved of command. *Reframe*: the Strike was never free.

**Act II, The Ledger (M4-M6).** Kaz, then Lyra, then Kaz. The war from the other side. The player helps the Wake rise, burns a town for a lie, learns what the Wake is for, and watches a Warlord stand in a ring of rising light and choose. *Reframe*: Class Nine means the Wake.

**Act III, The Closing (M7-M9).** Lyra, Kaz, then Idris: each hero's last act answers their flaw. The hedger commits everything. The obedient man carries a load nobody ordered. The martyr asks for company and spends one life, not nine thousand. *Reframe*: the villain was right about the sum and wrong about who pays.

## 8. The nine missions

Hero split is 3/3/3: Idris M1, M3, M9; Lyra M2, M5, M7; Kaz M4, M6, M8. All seeds 1101-1109 (hubs, camps and posts are identical on every seed).

**Engine facts that shape the missions** (read from the code, not guessed):
- `newGame` always creates a Hub and six Haulers for both sides before `campaignInit`, and `applySetup` cannot remove them. A mission that wants "no base" must make the base inert (restrict every building and unit type, start c0 e0, `foe.ai:false` where the foe must be scripted), not absent.
- `strike` takes its side as the **8th array element**: `['strike',tx,ty,r,dmg,delay,sfx|null,'me'|'enemy']`. The default is `'enemy'`, which damages the PLAYER's units and creeps (`updateZones` calls `aoeEnemies(z.team,...)`). **Every strike in this campaign carries an explicit side.**
- `trade >= n` reads `tradeEarn`, which is CRYSTALS earned by Freighter legs (0.6 x path length x demand x trade multiplier outbound, 0.25 x path length x multiplier on the return), not a count of runs.
- `aimod waveMult 0` clamps to 0.05; to stop waves use `firstWave` 99999 and `intervalMult` 20. `aimod` is a silent no-op when `foe.ai` is false.
- Scripted `spawn` with order `idle` makes Haulers go mining; use `hold`. `spawn ... attack` sends units at the player's Hub.
- `lost me` counts buildings as well as units. `hero dead` is false until the hero has been trained and died.
